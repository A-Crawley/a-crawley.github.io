The pit is four feet deep, lined with split bark, and holds nothing. I have counted it twice. By my count, the village has no food.

It is a good day for counting. A zero has no disputes in it.

The notice went up overnight, on a board nobody put up. Cormac says he would have heard the hammer. Cormac says this about most things he did not hear. The board stands where the two paths cross, although the paths are not yet paths, and the notice is pinned at my eye height, which I take personally.

> **NOTICE 1.** The village is established. Our objective is food. Associates are invited to acquire it.
> Records and Reconciliation Associate: M. Dunnock. Please keep the numbers honest, or at least consistent.
> Leadership

I am M. Dunnock. I learn this at breakfast, from the notice, because there is no breakfast. I am the one of six who can count past a hundred without using her feet, and Leadership appears to have heard. I am issued a stick. It is ash, two feet long, with one face stripped for notches. No instructions come with it, which I count as a good sign. Nobody has had time to get them wrong.

Wilf Ambrose is the Food Acquisition Associate. His notice is a smaller sheet pinned under mine, as if embarrassed to be there. He reads it standing up and nods at it the way you nod at a dog that has bitten somebody else. Then he takes a sack.

"Where will you go?" I ask.

"Where the food is."

"Where is that?"

"I'll know when I'm standing in it." He goes up the east slope, which is the only slope.

I sit beside the pit with my stick. Hester Boyle, Timber Operations Lead, is at the edge of the trees explaining something to one of them. Cormac Tull, Infrastructure Delivery Partner, is building the third hut, or has built two walls of it and is standing in the middle to see how it feels. Nan Prewitt sits in the doorway of the first hut with her hands in her lap, which the notice does not mention. Pip, who is nine, lies on his stomach in the dust beside the pit, counting ants. He has been at it for an hour. I have checked his total against mine, and we do not agree about the ants.

At noon I count the pit again. Zero. I cut no notch. The stick and I are in agreement.

---

Wilf comes back at dusk. The sack hangs from his hand like a coat from a hook.

"How was it?" I say.

"A lot of walking."

"I need a number."

He holds out the sack. I look in it and record what I see, which is the inside of a sack. I have no notch for that. I put it in my head under _in: nothing_, and Wilf sits down on the pit lid, and Pip, whose ear is against the bark side of it, says, "There's one in there."

"There isn't," I say. "By my count."

"The wall's warm."

Wilf stands. I lift the lid.

It is a flat brown round, about the size of a palm, and it is warm in the way a loaf is warm, which I record separately, because a warm thing has come from somewhere. I have been beside this pit since noon. I looked away once, for the length of one sneeze, to pick up the stick where it had rolled. The lid was the same when I looked back. The pit was not.

"Mine?" says Hester, who has come over for the occasion.

"You were with a tree," says Cormac.

"It was a long conversation."

"It wasn't me," says Wilf. He opens the sack wider, in case we have missed something. We have not.

Pinned to the board when I go to look, in the last of the light, is a new sheet. It was not there at noon. It is ruled in columns, with a heading in capitals.

> **FORM 1: FOOD ACQUIRED.**
> Quantity: \_\_\_ Source: \_\_\_
> Entries without a source will be treated as sourcing issues and reviewed.

I read _reviewed_ twice.

I have a stick, a form, and a loaf with no mother. I could write _unknown_. I try the shape of it in my head. An unknown source is a hole in the books, and Leadership, which has gone to the trouble of printing a column, clearly means to fill it. If I leave the line blank, somebody else will put a name in it, and I would rather choose. There is only one person in this village whose job is to bring food in. If the line is blank, it is his job that is blank.

I write _1_. Under Source I write _W. Ambrose_.

He reads it over my shoulder. He reads slowly, and I let him.

"I didn't," he says.

"It's a form."

"You said it to Pip." He means _by my count_. I say it ahead of every figure I am sure of and nothing ahead of the ones I am not, and I had not known anyone was listening for the difference. "You didn't say it to me."

"No."

He looks at his name for some time. Then he folds the sack and puts it under his arm, as though it might be needed.

By the time I have cut the first notch the board has changed again. I did not see it change. Nobody did.

> **NOTICE 2.** Food acquired: 1. Leadership calls this a breakthrough and has scheduled a review. Associates are invited to acquire 2.

Pip is at the foot of the post, where there is a wooden lever set into the base, the length of a hand, with two words burnt into the wood beside it. LOOK UP. Under them, in smaller letters, a third line: _Not yet ready._ Cormac says it is a hinge. Pip takes it in both hands and pulls, and it does not move, and he pulls again, and then he does what I have seen no one in the village do. He tips his head back and looks at the sky for a long time.

"Anything?" I say.

"I don't know yet."

I look up as well, to be fair to him. It is evening. There is cloud. There is nothing in it that I can count.

"By my count, nothing," I say, and hear that I have said it, and cannot tell which of us I'm trying to convince.

The board wants two. Wilf leaves at first light with a sack and a name on a form, and no idea where the loaf came from. Tomorrow I will go with him. Someone should count what he walks past.

## Echoes for the game

- The first-food log line could carry the story's version: a warm loaf in an empty store, and a form with a Source column that must not be left blank.
- "Entries without a source will be treated as sourcing issues and reviewed." Fits a Forager flavour line, or the stage 1 performance review.
- The wooden lever burnt with LOOK UP and "Not yet ready." A direct cousin of the locked button; the unlock log could say someone has stopped saying it is a hinge.
- "Please keep the numbers honest, or at least consistent." A records-related notice, or an achievement description.
- A villager who says "By my count" only before the figures they are sure of: a tic the log could give to a recurring name.
