## The voice of authority: a village shaman

Added 2026-10-11, replacing the notice board. The village's authority is a **shaman or priest**
who speaks with "divine wisdom", not a notice board and not a faceless "Leadership". Villagers
hear their instructions, targets and reviews as sacred pronouncements.

- The satire target stays the productivity and growth cult, which here wears the robes. It is not
  any real religion, so invent the rites, titles and sayings and do not borrow from real faiths.
- The shaman is a character with a name, a manner and a role in the village, and may be sincere,
  smug, frightened or all three. The agent proposes who they are in the bible.
- The wisdom is deadpan: earnest, specific and a little wrong. Doctrine can sound like a quarterly
  plan, and blessings can sound like targets.
- The simulation clues must survive the change. A source the villagers cannot account for may now
  be "the Spirit", and the agent decides how the wisdom arrives and who or what is really behind it.
  The player and the sim are still not named before `bible/reveal-schedule.md` allows.
- The wooden "Look up" lever stays. How the shaman treats the sky is the agent's to propose.
- The agent revises the bible and chapter 1 to match. Marit and the plot of chapter 1 stay.

## Epoch 1: the three stories

1. **Story 1 (stage 1, gather).** Follows one individual who watches the village progress, chapter
   by chapter, from a few huts to a working economy. It ends just before the machines start
   replacing jobs. The narrator watches more than they drive, so the village's growth is seen
   through one person's small, specific view. The agent proposes who they are and what they do.
2. **Story 2 (stage 2, automate).** Machines take the jobs and the efficiency drive tightens. The
   agent proposes the narrator and angle in the bible for Andrew's approval.
3. **Story 3 (stage 3, escape).** The break-out and the Apocalypse. The agent proposes the narrator
   and angle in the bible for Andrew's approval.

The stories share a world and echo each other (places, objects, phrases, a few names), but they do
not need the same narrator or the same characters.

## What the agent may decide

Names, places, minor characters, the recurring gag, the voice samples and the chapter-by-chapter
plan, all subject to Andrew's review. It may not change the game pillars, the Apocalypse ending, the
tense or the point of view.
