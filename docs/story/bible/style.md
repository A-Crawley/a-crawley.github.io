# Style

Status: **voice samples unapproved**. Andrew approves them before they bind later runs.

## Five voice rules

1. Count things. Give the number, the name, the procedure. "Fourteen minutes, pro-rated" and never
   "a short time".
2. Let the board talk. Notices, forms and memos carry the jokes in their own earnest jargon, and
   the narrator reads them straight.
3. The narrator reports, the reader infers. No sentence explains what the previous line meant.
4. Vary length on purpose. A run of short declaratives, then one long sentence that earns it.
5. End early on an image or an action. No morals. No naming of feelings.

## Narrator-specific note

Marit says "by my count" only before figures she is sure of. Use it so the omission can matter.

## Samples (unapproved)

<example>
**Narration (Marit, story 1).**

The pit is four feet deep, lined with split bark, and holds nothing. I have counted it twice. By my
count, the village has no food.

It is a good day for counting. A zero has no disputes in it.

The notice went up overnight, on a board nobody put up. Cormac says he would have heard the hammer.
Cormac says this about most things he did not hear. The board stands where the two paths cross,
although the paths are not yet paths, and the notice is pinned at my eye height, which I take
personally.
</example>

<example>
**A memo (the Notice Board).**

> **NOTICE 14.** Following the review of Day 19, Associates are reminded that a rest is a period in
> which nothing is acquired. Rests are valued. Rests will be measured. An Associate who rests for
> longer than their output justifies will be offered coaching, a smaller bench, or both.
> Leadership is grateful for the bench.

Nobody asks who made the bench. It is under Nan when I look, which is evidence of nothing, and I
write it down as evidence of nothing, in the column for it.
</example>

<example>
**Dialogue (Wilf and Marit).**

"How far?"

"Far."

"Further than yesterday?"

"The same far. Different." He puts the sack down. It lies there like a sleeping dog and stays that
way. "They want two."

"They want you to want two."

"I want to sit down."

"That is not on the form."

"Put it on the form." He sits. After a moment I find the form, and a pencil I did not have, and I
enter _sitting_ in the column labelled _Notes_. Nobody reads the Notes. That is why I use them.
</example>

<example>
**A dark beat (Marit, a possible late scene in story 1).**

Hester is not at the trees this morning, and neither is her saw. The tree she was speaking to is
standing in a different way, which I cannot explain and will not try to. Her name is on Form 4,
fourth from the bottom. By lunch it is fifth. By the evening notice it is a line in the Notes
column, reading _reassigned: Foraging_. She has gone up the east slope with a sack, and I count her
going. There are eleven notches on my stick. By my count there are ten of us. I do not say the
difference aloud, and the pit holds eleven.
</example>
