# Style

Status: **voice samples unapproved**, and rewritten on 2026-10-11 for the village-shaman register.
Andrew approves them before they bind later runs.

## Five voice rules

1. Count things. Give the number, the name, the procedure. "A Hearing at the third bell, pro-rated
   for the short" and never "a meeting soon".
2. Let the Speaker talk. Sayings, blessings and Hearings carry the jokes in their own earnest
   sacred-plan jargon, and the narrator reports them straight. A blessing is a target. Doctrine
   reads like a quarterly plan. No real faith's words, rites, calendar or scripture.
3. The narrator reports, the reader infers. No sentence explains what the previous line meant.
4. Vary length on purpose. A run of short declaratives, then one long sentence that earns it.
5. End early on an image or an action. No morals. No naming of feelings.

## The Speaker's register

- On the Stump: matched pairs, a figure, "Hear this, and hear it as a figure", "so it is tallied".
  No "I". The Spirit is the subject of every verb that matters.
- Off the Stump: plainer, lower, shorter. He sounds tired. This is where the sympathy lives.
- The wisdom is a little wrong in a specific way: it treats a price as a plan, a number as a mercy.
- Do not make it mystical. No incense, no visions, no cosmic language. It is bookkeeping with a
  cloak on.

## Narrator-specific note

Marit says "by my count" only before figures she is sure of. Use it so the omission can matter.
She is not a believer. She does not mock the Speaker; she records what she has been told.

## Samples (unapproved)

<example>
**Narration (Marit, story 1).**

The pit is four feet deep, lined with split bark, and holds nothing. I have counted it twice. By my
count, the village has no food.

It is a good day for counting. A zero has no disputes in it.

The Speaker is already on the Stump when I climb out. Nobody saw him arrive. Cormac says he would
have heard the climbing, and Cormac says this about most things he did not hear. Dunstan Fallow
stands where the two paths cross, although the paths are not yet paths, in a cloak of sacking, with
his eyes shut and his head tilted like a man listening at a wall. His hand goes to his ear and stays
there.
</example>

<example>
**A Saying (the Speaker, from the Stump).**

> **SAYING 14.** Hear this, and hear it as a figure. A rest is a period in which nothing is
> acquired. Rests are valued. Rests will be measured. A Hand who rests longer than their Yield
> justifies shall be offered Counsel, a smaller bench, or both. The Spirit is grateful for the bench.
> May your rest be a third shorter than you feared, and may you be grateful for the third. So it is
> tallied.

Nobody asks who made the bench. It is under Nan when I look, which is evidence of nothing, and I
write it down as evidence of nothing, in the column for it.
</example>

<example>
**Dialogue (Wilf and Marit).**

"How far?"

"Far."

"Further than yesterday?"

"The same far. Different." He puts the sack down. It lies there like a sleeping dog and stays that
way. "The Spirit wants two."

"The Spirit leans toward two."

"I want to sit down."

"That is not in the Tally."

"Put it in the Tally." He sits. After a moment I find the bark, and a burnt twig I did not have, and
I enter _sitting_ in the column called Quiet. Nobody reads the Quiet. That is why I use it.
</example>

<example>
**The Speaker off the Stump (a private beat, a possible scene in story 1).**

After the dusk Saying Dunstan does not go to his hut. He sits on the edge of the Stump with the
cloak in his lap and counts the notches sewn on it, thirty-one, with a finger. His lips move. He
gets thirty-one each time.

"It says the number and the number," he tells me, not looking up. "I put the rest in."

"The rest of what?"

"The reasons." He folds the cloak over his knees. "Marit, is there a reason in thirty-one?"

"By my count it is thirty-one."

He nods as though I had said yes, and goes to bed holding his ear.
</example>

<example>
**A dark beat (Marit, a possible late scene in story 1).**

Hester is not at the trees this morning, and neither is her saw. The tree she was speaking to is
standing in a different way, which I cannot explain and will not try to. Her name is on the
Standings, fourth from the bottom. By lunch it is fifth. By the dusk Saying it is a line in the
Quiet, reading _reassigned: Foraging_. She has gone up the east slope with a sack, and I count her
going. There are eleven notches on my stick. By my count there are ten of us. I do not say the
difference aloud, and the pit holds eleven.
</example>
