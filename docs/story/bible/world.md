# World

## Logline

A village gets a notice board, a stick for counting and a plan. One person keeps the count while the
village grows into something that no longer needs her to.

## Tone statement

Dry, specific, deadpan. Institutions speak earnestly in their own jargon and the absurdity sits in
the content. Narrators never wink. Funny first, then unsettling, with the voice kept flat while the
content darkens. Villagers stay sympathetic. The satire aims at metrics and growth logic, never at a
villager's dignity.

## World rules

What the sim can and cannot do (story-facing, not for explanation in the text):

- The village runs on a clock the villagers cannot see. Days are numbered by the board, not by them.
- Things can appear without a carrier: food, notices, new forms, new arrivals. Nobody in the
  village treats this as strange, because "sourcing" and "onboarding" cover it.
- Nobody has a past older than the first day. Asked, they answer in admin terms ("onboarding").
- The sky repeats itself. Clouds are reused. The weather is always adequate.
- The sim cannot do real interiority for the villagers beyond what they say and do. Villagers are
  sympathetic in practice, not in explanation.

What villagers do not know: that they are in a simulation, that "Leadership" is a single presence
who sometimes presses something, that the sky carries a counter, that there will be a rival.

How the drift shows up in daily life (never as a number, never named in the text):

- Efficiency side: the board's forms, ranking, "reviewed", rounding down for the weak, renaming rest
  as "idle hands", crediting someone for work they did not do so the books close.
- Compassion side: rounding up, topping up a ration, a "Notes" column nobody reads, rest.
- The drift is always seen as a small act by a named person. Leadership's notices answer it
  afterwards in approving language.

## Recurring gag and objects

- **The Notice Board.** Appears in the night. Notices are signed "Leadership". It praises anything
  that goes up and schedules a review for anything that goes wrong.
- **The tally stick** (Marit's, story 1). A spare notch: the pit always holds one more than she has
  counted. Leadership calls it growth. Returns in stories 2 and 3.
- **"By my count."** Marit says it only before figures she is sure of. Later narrators may inherit
  it as a village idiom, losing the meaning.
- **The LOOK UP lever** burnt into the base of the notice board post, with "Not yet ready." The
  village says it is a hinge.
- **"Associates are invited to ..."** the board's way of giving orders.
- **Warm things with no carrier.** Food that is warm when it should not be.
