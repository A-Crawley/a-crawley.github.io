# Cast

Dials run 1 to 10: proactivity (P), likeability (L), competence (C). "Start" is at the first chapter
of the person's story, "End" at the last.

The village on Day 1 is seven: Marit, Wilf, Hester, Cormac, Nan, Pip and Dunstan. Four newcomers in
chapter 3 make eleven.

## Story 1

### Marit Dunnock (narrator, story 1)

Keeper of the Tally (blessed with the office of Records and Reconciliation). Keeps the count with
an ash tally stick. Not a believer, and not a sceptic either. She records that she has been told.

- **Want:** a count that closes, with no difference to explain.
- **Flaw:** she would rather record than speak. She edits the numbers to avoid a scene, and tells
  herself it is accuracy.
- **Fear:** that the figure she is sure of turns out not to matter.
- **Diction:** exact, dry, counts things. Short declaratives, one long sentence per paragraph at
  most. Says "by my count" only before figures she is sure of. Never rounds aloud.
- **Never says aloud:** that she is lonely, and that she looks up when nobody is watching.
- **Dials:** P 3 to 5, L 7 to 6, C 8 to 8. Start: P 3, L 7, C 8. End: P 5, L 6, C 8.
- **Where dials move:** P to 4 in ch1 (decides to follow Wilf), to 5 in ch3 (rounds Hester's figure
  on purpose). L to 6 in ch3 (a small unkind rounding she does not undo).

### Dunstan Fallow, the Speaker of the Yield

The village's shaman. Tall, stooped to hear, in a cloak of sacking sewn with a notch for every
Saying, which is already too heavy and will get heavier. Stands on the Stump of Saying at dawn and
dusk with his eyes shut and his head tilted, and speaks what he has received. His hand goes to his
ear when he says "the Spirit". He does not know this.

His name is a farming word for land left unplanted. He is the one who forbids rest.

- **Role:** he is the village's only line to the Spirit. He speaks Sayings, opens and closes Hearings,
  blesses the Hands in the morning and reads the Standings in the evening. He has no listed task,
  and does not need one, because he is the task.
- **Want:** a Saying about him. In all the Words he has received there has not been one. He wants
  the Spirit to say his name, once, in a sentence that is not an instruction.
- **Flaw:** he cannot say "I do not know". Whatever he is given, he supplies a reason, a blessing
  and a figure. When the Word is only a number he builds a doctrine round it, and the village then
  has to live in the doctrine.
- **Fear:** that the Word stops. Beneath that: that the Word is only ever numbers and he has been
  the one adding the meaning.
- **Diction rule:** speaks in matched pairs and finishes on a figure. Opens a Saying with "Hear
  this, and hear it as a figure". Blesses with a target: "May your yield be a third above your last
  yield, and may you be grateful for the third." Closes with "so it is tallied". Never uses the
  word "I" in a Saying; the Spirit is the subject. Off the Stump he is plainer, lower and sounds
  tired.
- **Never says aloud:** "I do not know what it means." And that he looks at the Margin at night
  with the Latch behind him, waiting to be told the next thing.
- **Sympathetic because:** he is the only one in the village who is audited by what he says. He
  gives his own Portion away when no one is looking, and calls it "a Portion returned to the Yield".
- **Dials:** Start P 7, L 6, C 3. End of story 1: P 8, L 4, C 4.
- **Where dials move:** L to 5 in ch2 (turns the spare notch into doctrine and is applauded for it).
  P to 8 and L to 4 in ch3 (reads the Standings aloud, to the whole village, in order). C to 4 in
  ch4 (he knows the pit is exactly right before Marit tells him, and does not say how).

### Wilf Ambrose

Gatherer of the First Rank (food acquisition). Laconic, literal. Describes the job as "a lot of
walking". Wants to be left to walk. Flaw: takes credit as he takes blame, silently. Fear: being the
blank on the Tally. Diction: four words where one would do and none of them decoration. Never says:
that he likes it. Dials: P 3, L 8, C 5 at start; P 4, L 8, C 6 at end of story 1.

### Hester Boyle

Warden of Timber (timber operations lead). Talks to trees and asks them for their views. Wants a
tree to answer. Flaw: vague on numbers. Fear: being ranked. Diction: addresses a plant before a
person. Never says: that she is the best woodcutter in the village. Dials: P 6, L 7, C 7.

### Cormac Tull

Raiser of Walls (infrastructure delivery). Builds. Says he would have heard it, whatever "it" is.
Wants to be asked what the huts are for. Flaw: certainty without evidence. Fear: an unlabelled
drawing. Diction: "I'd have heard that." Never says: that he cannot hear the Word when it is
spoken. Dials: P 7, L 6, C 6.

### Nan Prewitt

The oldest villager, has no listed office. Sits in doorways. Wants a bench in the sun. Flaw: gives
away her share. Fear: being a line item. Diction: calls everyone "love" and means the opposite when
she is displeased. Never says: that the light was different once. Dials: P 2, L 9, C 8. Dunstan
does not bless Nan. He does not know what to say to her, because she was not Received with a task.

### Pip Ashgrove

Nine years old. Counts ants. Looks up. Wants to know what the lever is for. Flaw: believes people
when they say nothing is there, until he checks. Fear: the answer. Diction: states what he sees
without adjectives. Never says: what the number says. Dials: P 8, L 9, C 5. Returns as the narrator
of story 3 (see below), grown.

### The Spirit

Never appears, never speaks to anyone but Dunstan. Known only through what the Speaker says it said.
Gives, approves, schedules a Hearing, leans toward two. Treats a price as a plan. Not named as
anything but the Spirit before the reveal schedule allows.

## Proposed narrators for stories 2 and 3 (for Andrew's approval)

### Story 2: Odile Fenn, Hand of the Third Rank, supervising a machine (proposed)

A former forager "retrained to supervise a machine". The angle: she watches her old job done by an
automated forager and writes the Morning Counsel notes for it. The Counsel is the Speaker's
sunrise gathering, kept in the three-part form "What was Yielded, What shall be Yielded, What
Stands in the Way". Want: to be of use. Flaw: she is easy to convince that being kept on is a
mercy. Fear: being the supervisor of nothing. Voice: Counsel-note English, devout at the surface,
with the sentence that matters buried in the third part. Dunstan blesses the machine ("Be Fruitful,
and be Serviced") and is visibly troubled that it needs no blessing. Her story ends with the Word
arriving in a second voice, not Dunstan's, through something that is not a person.

### Story 3: Pip Ashgrove, Keeper of the Latch (proposed)

The boy who looked up, grown. The angle: he converts the village's infrastructure into ways to open
the Latch because he was always going to be the one to open it. Dunstan is old by now and the Word
is thinner for him. Want: to see the thing he was told was not there. Flaw: single-mindedness.
Fear: being right. Voice: flat observation in the tradition of his childhood, precise, patient, no
adjectives he cannot defend. Story 3 ends with the Latch pulled and the Apocalypse. The last image
answers Marit's first: Dunstan at the Stump with his eyes open, and nothing to receive, giving the
village its first honest Saying, which is the number zero.
