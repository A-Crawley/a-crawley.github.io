# Cast

Dials run 1 to 10: proactivity (P), likeability (L), competence (C). "Start" is at the first chapter
of the person's story, "End" at the last.

## Story 1

### Marit Dunnock (narrator, story 1)

Records and Reconciliation Associate. Keeps the count with an ash tally stick.

- **Want:** a count that closes, with no difference to explain.
- **Flaw:** she would rather record than speak. She edits the numbers to avoid a scene, and tells
  herself it is accuracy.
- **Fear:** that the figure she is sure of turns out not to matter.
- **Diction:** exact, dry, counts things. Short declaratives, one long sentence per paragraph at
  most. Says "by my count" only before figures she is sure of. Never rounds aloud.
- **Never says aloud:** that she is lonely, and that she looks up when nobody is watching.
- **Dials:** P 3 to 5, L 7 to 6, C 8 to 8. Start: P 3, L 7, C 8. End: P 5, L 6, C 8.
- **Where dials move:** P to 4 in ch1 (decides to follow Wilf), to 5 in ch3 (rounds Hester's figure
  on purpose). L to 6 in ch3 (a small unkind rounding she does not undo).

### Wilf Ambrose

Food Acquisition Associate. Laconic, literal. Describes the job as "a lot of walking". Wants to be
left to walk. Flaw: takes credit as he takes blame, silently. Fear: being the blank on the form.
Diction: four words where one would do and none of them decoration. Never says: that he likes it.
Dials: P 3, L 8, C 5 at start; P 4, L 8, C 6 at end of story 1.

### Hester Boyle

Timber Operations Lead. Talks to trees and asks them for their views. Wants a tree to answer.
Flaw: vague on numbers. Fear: being ranked. Diction: addresses a plant before a person. Never says:
that she is the best woodcutter in the village. Dials: P 6, L 7, C 7.

### Cormac Tull

Infrastructure Delivery Partner. Builds. Says he would have heard it, whatever "it" is. Wants to be
asked what the huts are for. Flaw: certainty without evidence. Fear: an unlabelled drawing.
Diction: "I'd have heard that." Never says: that he cannot read the notices. Dials: P 7, L 6, C 6.

### Nan Prewitt

The oldest villager, has no listed job. Sits in doorways. Wants a bench in the sun. Flaw: gives
away her share. Fear: being a line item. Diction: calls everyone "love" and means the opposite
when she is displeased. Never says: that the light was different once. Dials: P 2, L 9, C 8.

### Pip Ashgrove

Nine years old. Counts ants. Looks up. Wants to know what the lever is for. Flaw: believes people
when they say nothing is there, until he checks. Fear: the answer. Diction: states what he sees
without adjectives. Never says: what the number says. Dials: P 8, L 9, C 5. Returns as the narrator
of story 3 (see below), grown.

### Leadership

Never appears. Notices only. Writes in approving language. Treats a price as a plan.

## Proposed narrators for stories 2 and 3 (for Andrew's approval)

### Story 2: Odile Fenn, Machine Supervisor (proposed)

A former forager "retrained to supervise a machine". The angle: she watches her old job done by an
automated forager and writes the standup notes for it. Want: to be of use. Flaw: she is easy to
convince that being kept on is a kindness. Fear: being the supervisor of nothing. Voice: standup
and release-note English, cheerful at the surface, with the sentence that matters buried in the
third bullet. Her story ends with the sim visibly cracking and a second set of notices in a different
hand.

### Story 3: Pip Ashgrove, Exploit Technician (proposed)

The boy who looked up, grown. The angle: he converts the village's infrastructure into exploits
because he was always going to be the one to open the lever. Want: to see the thing he was told was
not there. Flaw: single-mindedness. Fear: being right. Voice: flat observation in the tradition of
his childhood, precise, patient, no adjectives he cannot defend. Story 3 ends with the lever pulled
and the Apocalypse, and a last image that answers Marit's first (a count of a thing that is zero).
